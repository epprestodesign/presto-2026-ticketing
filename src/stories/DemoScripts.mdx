{/*
  Demo Scripts — a presenting aid, not component documentation.

  WHY THIS IS ITS OWN TOP-LEVEL CATEGORY rather than a page under Getting
  Started. Everything else in this Storybook documents what a thing IS: props,
  states, variants, the spec it was built from. This documents how to SHOW one,
  out loud, to a room, in sixty seconds. That is a different kind of document
  with a different reader — someone who is about to present, often about to
  share their screen — so it gets its own home near the top where it can be
  found under pressure rather than being filed among the orientation docs.

  The four scripts are ONE PAGE on purpose. These prototypes are demoed back to
  back far more often than singly, and a presenter mid-demo wants to scroll, not
  navigate. One page is also one file to keep true when a prototype changes.

  THE CONTENT IS GROUNDED IN THE CODE, not in the surface. Every figure quoted
  below — $259/night, the 6-credential cap, the $0 "make your own way" option —
  was read out of the prototype's own data files, so the script survives a
  follow-up question. If a prototype's pricing or flow changes, these numbers
  are the part that goes stale; check them against the app before presenting.
*/}

{/*
  WHY THIS STYLE BLOCK EXISTS — don't delete it, the page silently loses its
  typography without it.

  MDX applies markdown processing to the children of a block-level JSX element
  when they sit on their own lines, so this:

      <h1 style={{ fontSize: '2.5rem' }}>
        Four Prototypes, Four Minutes
      </h1>

  compiles to an <h1> containing a <p>. The <h1> keeps the 2.5rem, but the text
  lives in that inner <p>, and Storybook's own docs stylesheet (.sbdocs p) sets
  it to 14px — so the headline rendered at body size while the element measured
  40px. Every multi-line block on this page hits the same trap, including all
  twenty spoken lines.

  One rule fixes the class of problem rather than each instance: inside this
  page, a generated <p> inherits from whatever styled element wraps it. Put the
  intent on the container, as the components below do, and it now lands.
*/}

  </Beat>
  <Beat t="0:11" does={<>Browse hotels, open <strong>Rosen Centre</strong>, reserve a room.</>}>
    Rosen Centre, a tenth of a mile from the Convention Center, two-fifty-nine a night across three

  </Beat>

  </Beat>
  <Beat t="0:52" does={<>Jump to the <strong>confirmation</strong>.</>}>

<Script
  n="03"
  name="trip-builder"
  port="http://localhost:7100/?screen=landing"
  title="There is no step one"
  premise={<>The awkward guest: parking only, or tickets without a room. <strong style={{ fontStyle: 'normal', color: C.text }}>The cart is the state</strong> — there's no stepper to be halfway through.</>}
>
  <Beat t="0:00" does={<>Open the <strong>landing screen</strong>. Gesture across the three doors.</>}>
    Three ways in — hotel, tickets, or an add-on. Each one is a first step and none of them is the
    first step. That's the whole idea: this flow has no order to be out of.
  </Beat>
  <Beat t="0:13" does={<>Choose <strong>Start with an add-on</strong>. Add a shuttle.</>}>
    So let's do the awkward one on purpose. A shuttle pass, no room, no tickets. In a stepper this
    guest has skipped two steps and looks broken. Here they've just got a trip with one thing in it —
    which is a real thing people buy.
  </Beat>
  <Beat t="0:29" does={<>Point at the <strong>trip bar</strong>, then add a hotel from it.</>}>
    The bar across the top replaces the progress rail. It doesn't tell you how far along you are — it
    tells you what you're holding, and offers all three doors on every screen. Adding a hotel is the
    same gesture from anywhere.
  </Beat>
  <Beat t="0:44" isKey does={<>Open the <strong>cart peek</strong> from the nav. Then point at the address bar.</>}>
    Nights and rooms are edited on the line itself, next to the total they move — same as every other
    quantity in the cart. And the whole trip is in the URL. So a half-built trip is a link: send someone
    tickets-only at checkout and they open exactly that, which is the state worth arguing about in a review.
  </Beat>
  <Beat t="0:56" does={<>Continue to <strong>checkout</strong>.</>}>
    Checkout takes whatever's in the cart. One item or six.
  </Beat>
  <Close>Nothing here can be half-finished, because there was never an order to finish.</Close>
</Script>
